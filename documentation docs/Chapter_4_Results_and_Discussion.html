Chapter 4: Empirical Results and Technical Discussion
Quantitative Ranging Benchmarks, 33-Session Spatial Evaluation, Sim2Real Transfer & Kalman Kinematic Smoothing

4.1 Overview and Experimental Objectives

This chapter presents the quantitative empirical results of the Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) indoor positioning system developed and formalized in Chapter 3. The experimental evaluation is organized around three hierarchical layers of system performance:

  1. Layer 1: 1D Ranging & Attenuation Estimation — Comparative benchmark of classical radio propagation physics models against 19 parametric and ensemble machine learning architectures trained on 66 spatial, statistical, and temporal features.
  2. Layer 2: 2D Spatial Coordinate Positioning — Evaluation of multilateration solvers versus direct non-linear coordinate regression, evaluated under both descriptive in-sample replay and strict zero-leakage session-grouped 5-fold cross-validation.
  3. Layer 3: Kinematic Trajectory Smoothing & Latency — Empirical assessment of Kalman filtering on high-frequency multipath jitter dampening, step response settling time, and runtime execution latency.

All results reported in this chapter are derived from physical hardware acquisitions using ESP32-WROOM-32 anchors and calibrated BLE 5.0 beacons, verified through cryptographic model serialization hashes and automated regression test suites.

Disaggregating positioning accuracy by physical coordinate locations reveals a critical structural phenomenon:

This boundary degradation is governed by two complementary physical and mathematical drivers:

  • Geometric Dilution of Precision (GDOP) Expansion: As a tag approaches or breaches the perimeter of the anchor rectangle, the angles subtended by receiver lines-of-sight collapse, causing positional covariance to expand rapidly.
  • Regression Centroid Shrinkage: In decision-tree ensembles (ExtraTrees, Random Forest), predictions represent the mean of terminal leaf training instances. For extreme boundary points near (5.0,5.0)m or (1.0,1.0)m, out-of-fold trees pull predictions toward the empirical centroid of the training data (x¯≈2.85m,y¯≈2.70m).

The pipeline enforces idempotent packet deduplication (Δt≤50ms) with zero duplicate writes, preserving telemetry ground truth and computing real-time tracking error vectors (e→=[xest−xtrue,yest−ytrue]).

GDOP=Tr((HTH)−1)
  • Excellent (GDOP≤2.5): Symmetrical anchor constellation; optimal interior tracking.
  • Good (2.5<GDOP≤5.0): Standard multi-anchor coverage; sub-meter precision.
  • Moderate (5.0<GDOP≤10.0): Asymmetric or obstructed geometry; increased variance.
  • Poor / Collinear (GDOP>10.0): Collinear or boundary geometry; fallback engaged.

4.6 Explicit Scope Boundaries and Non-Implemented Extensions

To preserve strict academic integrity, this dissertation explicitly documents potential methodologies that were evaluated during preliminary scoping but not implemented in the completed experimental system:

  1. Fingerprinting / K-Nearest Neighbors (K-NN) RSSI Map Matching:
  • Status: Outside Completed Experimental Scope.
  • Rationale: Radio fingerprinting requires extensive, labor-intensive point-by-point offline grid surveys. Any structural modification (e.g., furniture relocation, partition movement) invalidates the radio map, necessitating complete re-surveying. The completed research specifically investigates parametric feature-engineered regression and direct spatial coordinate mapping, which generalize across continuous metric spaces without discrete grid survey overhead.
  1. 3D Vertical Altitude (Z-Axis) Localization:
  • Status: Outside Completed Experimental Scope.
  • Rationale: All 4 physical ESP32 anchor nodes were deployed in a coplanar configuration at tripod height H=0.96m. Coplanar anchor geometry causes the vertical Dilution of Precision (VDOP) to diverge asymptotically (VDOP→∞), creating an unresolvable geometric singularity in the Z-axis. Consequently, tracking is strictly bounded to the horizontal 2D plane: (x,y)∈[0.0,5.0]×[0.0,5.0]m.
  1. Commercial Multi-Room and Industrial Building Deployment:
  • Status: Outside Completed Experimental Scope.
  • Rationale: As established in Chapter 3 (Section 3.3.1), the empirical investigation was conducted within a controlled 5.0m×5.0m physical testbed established in a backyard environment as a small-scale proof of concept. The resulting positioning metrics reflect performance within this surveyed test area. Performance across complex industrial facilities, multi-room offices, shopping centres, and warehouses with heavy metal racking or through-wall propagation was not directly established and remains designated for future operational field trials.

4.7 Chapter Summary

The quantitative experiments presented in this chapter demonstrate:

  1. Feature-engineered gradient boosted regression reduces 1D distance estimation error by 72.4 compared to calibrated logarithmic path loss physics (1.2734m vs. 4.6139m MAE on N=7,471 held-out windows).
  2. Strict out-of-fold session cross-validation (GroupKFold, K=5) on 1,961 multi-anchor observation windows establishes a realistic generalization median 2D positioning error of 0.4059m (with 69.96 of predictions achieving sub-meter accuracy), eliminating the artificial optimism of naive in-sample replay (0.0244m median).
  3. Direct non-linear coordinate regression statistically outperforms two-stage ranging multilateration (0.8853m vs. 0.9107m mean error, paired t=−3.106, p=0.00192), avoiding error compounding through intermediate trilateration geometry.
  4. Error tier stratification demonstrates that 76.08 of all predictions fall within 1.5m, with central testbed tracking achieving a median accuracy of 0.0910m (98.3 sub-50cm). Large errors (>2.5m) are strictly localized to perimeter and corner boundary points due to geometric dilation of precision (GDOP) and tree-ensemble regression shrinkage.
  5. Discrete proximity zone classification achieves 92.15 accuracy and 0.9216 F1-score, providing robust coarse geofencing during severe RF fading.
  6. Kinematic Kalman filtering dampens trajectory jitter by up to 70.2 with negligible computational overhead (0.062ms per update; total pipeline latency 1.809ms), supporting real-time tracking at 10Hz beacon rates.
  7. Real-time hardware-in-the-loop simulation benchmarks closely mirror physical testbed dynamics (MAE=0.8751m vs. 0.8853m), with automated GDOP indexing and a 4-stage graceful fallback ladder ensuring continuous operational reliability.
  8. All four core research hypotheses (H1−H4) are statistically and empirically confirmed, while discrete fingerprinting, 3D altitude resolution, and commercial-scale multi-room deployments are formally delimited as outside the completed scope.
