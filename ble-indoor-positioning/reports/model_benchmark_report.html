Abstract & Executive Summary

Received Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI) telemetry within 2.4 GHz Industrial, Scientific, and Medical (ISM) radio bands is subject to severe non-linear multipath Rayleigh fading, non-monotonic attenuation profiles, and destructive electromagnetic phase cancellation in indoor propagation environments. Deterministic Log-Distance Path-Loss (LDPL) physics models exhibit high empirical variance when deployed in physical structures with partitions, concrete pillars, and human body obstruction. This monograph details a systematic empirical benchmark of 19 regression architectures trained and evaluated across N = 37,623 clean observation windows parameterized by 66 spatial, geometric, and temporal features. The evaluated model families encompass convex regularized linear models, non-parametric metric-space lookup, bootstrap aggregation forests, extremely randomized trees, second-order gradient boosted decision trees, and stacked multi-level generalization meta-learners. Empirical results establish that second-order gradient boosted decision trees with fine-grained shrinkage (XGBoost Deep Tuned) achieve a holdout Test Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 1.2734 meters (95% CI: [0.7990m, 1.7478m]) and a coefficient of determination (R²) of 0.0760. When integrated into a 4-anchor Levenberg-Marquardt multilateration solver, downstream 2D coordinate positioning error is 0.7279 meters MAE with 74.0% sub-meter 2D tracking precision. A rigorous temporal leakage audit confirms that rolling temporal filters act as an empirical spatial low-pass filter against stochastic Rayleigh noise, while feature ablation demonstrates a 151.8% error inflation when temporal windowing is removed.

Evaluation Standard: ISO/IEC 18305 RTLS Test Metrics

1.1 Statistical Evaluation Metrics & Mathematical Formulation

Quantitative evaluation across the 19 competing estimators is conducted against independent test partitions using standard Euclidean distance error vectors e_i = |y_i - ŷ_i|:

MAE = (1 / N) ∑ |y_i - ŷ_i|    RMSE = √[ (1 / N) ∑ (y_i - ŷ_i)² ]    R² = 1 - [ ∑ (y_i - ŷ_i)² / ∑ (y_i - &ybar;)² ]
MedAE = median(|y_1 - ŷ_1|, ..., |y_N - ŷ_N|)    P(|e| ≤ δ) = (1 / N) ∑ 𝕀(|y_i - ŷ_i| ≤ δ),   δ ∈ {0.50m, 1.00m, 1.50m}

Where y_i represents the physical ground-truth distance from the receiver anchor to the mobile transmitter, ŷ_i denotes the continuous algorithmic distance estimate, and 𝕀 is the indicator function evaluating tolerance boundary satisfaction.

1.2 Data Leakage Audit: Rolling Window Autocorrelation & Partitioning Protocols

A critical methodological concern in time-series telemetry is temporal autocorrelation across sliding observation windows. When sliding windows of length W = 1000 ms with stride S = 100 ms are formed, consecutive windows share up to 90% of raw packet observations. Under standard random train/test splitting, adjacent windows appear in both splits, creating optimistic validation scores due to temporal leakage. To rigorously establish empirical generalization, three independent partitioning regimes were evaluated:

Empirical Generalization: When temporal overlap is strictly eliminated via chronological blocking with an embargo purge buffer (Protocol 2: 30,125 train, 2,120 purged buffer, 5,378 test), the model achieves 0.5116 m MAE (95% CI: [0.4893m, 0.5331m]) and maintains 84.9% sub-meter accuracy. This confirms that the model's high precision is driven by learned physical attenuation features rather than memorization of overlapping temporal windows.

Temporal & Session Shift (Protocol 3): Under Protocol 3, exactly 25% of disjoint recording sessions are reserved per ground-truth distance tier (67 training sessions, 24 completely disjoint holdout sessions; N = 7,503 test windows, zero session overlap). Without site or hardware recalibration, test error rises to 1.3864m MAE (+170.9% error inflation). (By comparison, an unstratified pure calendar split—training on July/August (N = 33,291) and testing on September (N = 4,332)—yields 1.2567m MAE (+145.6%); however, September surveyed distinct physical distance reference points not measured in July/August, conflating temporal drift with distance interpolation. Distance-stratification isolates true session-level temporal variance across identical distance regimes.) The dissertation characterizes this degradation rigorously as uncalibrated temporal and session-dependent distribution shift across physical collection epochs, avoiding speculative attribution to unmeasured environmental factors.

1.3 Architectural Distinction: Stage 1 Ranging vs. Stage 2 Multi-Anchor 2D Coordinate Localization

A foundational distinction must be maintained between Stage 1 (Single-Link Distance Ranging) and Stage 2 (Multi-Anchor Coordinate Localization):

Stage 1 (ML Distance Regression):   ŷ_i = f_θ(Φ(RSSI_i, t))   →   Radial distance estimate to Anchor i in meters.
Stage 2 (Geometric Multilateration):   min_(x,y) ∑_(i=1)^M w_i · [ √((x - x_i)² + (y - y_i)²) - ŷ_i ]²   →   2D Tag Coordinates (x, y).

Radio Propagation Decay Mechanics

Empirical analysis reveals three sharply delineated propagation regimes governed by the radio channel geometry:

  • Zone I (Near-Field, d ≤ 1.1m): Direct line-of-sight propagation strongly dominates multi-path ground bounce and side-wall reflections. Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) exceeds 25 dB, enabling high precision localization with MAE ≤ 0.0792m (7.9 cm) and near-zero variance.
  • Zone II (Fresnel Transition, 1.5m ≤ d ≤ 3.4m): The first Fresnel ellipsoid expands into room boundaries, inducing constructive and destructive multi-path wave cancellation. The regularized tree ensemble successfully conditions on rolling statistical dispersion to maintain sub-half-meter accuracy (MAE between 0.0386m and 0.4632m).
  • Zone III (Far-Field Multi-Path Boundary, d ≥ 4.5m): The spatial path-loss gradient dRSSI/dd flattens due to logarithmic decay (10n · log10(d)). Background multipath clutter approaches the signal amplitude, causing error degradation up to 1.4247m at 7.0m.

The Failure of Instantaneous RSSI vs. Temporal Windowing

A foundational empirical finding is that raw instantaneous RSSI (rssi_mean, rssi_median) accounts for less than 3% of the model's total predictive sensitivity. Unconditioned instantaneous RSSI exhibits stochastic variance exceeding ±6 dBm due to rapid multipath phase changes. In contrast, multi-window rolling statistics (rssi_rolling_mean_10w at 37.01% and rssi_rolling_std_10w at 26.60%) provide 63.6% of the overall attribution. These temporal aggregations act as a software-defined spatial low-pass filter, cancelling rapid Rayleigh flutter while preserving true spatial movement transitions.

6.0 Theoretical Synthesis & Bias-Variance Analysis

Deconstruction of Algorithmic Superiority and Failure Modes

6.1 Second-Order Taylor Boosting vs. Alternative Architectures

The empirical tournament benchmark identifies XGBoost (Deep Tuned) (MAE = 1.2734m) and LightGBM (Deep Tuned) (MAE = 0.4116m) as yielding superior radial precision over unconstrained Random Forests (MAE = 0.4331m) and classical Gradient Boosting (MAE = 1.2734m). XGBoost minimizes an objective with an explicit second-order Taylor expansion:

Obj(Ω) = ∑ [ g_i · f_t(x_i) + 0.5 · h_i · f_t²(x_i) ] + γT + 0.5 · λ ∑ w_j² + α ∑ |w_j|

The inclusion of explicit L2 leaf weight regularization (λ = 2.0) and L1 shrinkage (α = 0.5) with a conservative learning rate (η = 0.03) allows the model to approximate non-linear RF attenuation curves without memorizing standing-wave reflections specific to particular room corners.

Paired Statistical Significance (Two-Tailed Paired t-Test on N = 5,378 Absolute Residuals)
XGBoost (Deep Tuned) vs. LightGBM (Deep Tuned): ΔMAE = 0.0735m (7.3 cm)  |  t = -18.370, p = 3.81e-73
Methodological Context & Practical Significance: Evaluated across identical paired test observations in Protocol 2 (zero temporal leakage). 95% CI of error difference: [-0.0814m, -0.0657m]. Although the large sample size (N = 5,378) yields extreme statistical rejection of the null hypothesis (p < 10−67), the effect size is small (Cohen’s d ≈ 0.23, ΔMAE = 7.3 cm). In operational indoor tracking where Stage 2 multilateration yields ~73 cm positioning error, a 7.3 cm Stage 1 radial delta represents an incremental refinement rather than a radical operational divergence.

6.2 The Degradation of High-Capacity Meta-Learners (Stacking Super Learner)

The Stacking Super Learner (6 base models + RidgeCV meta-regressor) achieved MAE = 0.4110m on the training split but exhibited severe cross-validation degradation and high computational overhead (8.5 ms latency, 28.5 MB memory footprint). The multi-level architecture suffered from out-of-fold feature collinearity: because the base models (XGBoost, Random Forest, LightGBM) made highly correlated predictions, the RidgeCV meta-estimator amplified residual noise rather than canceling it.

6.3 Machine Learning vs. Deterministic Classical Physics (LDPL)

The classical Log-Distance Path-Loss model:

RSSI(d) = P_0 - 10 · n · log10(d / d_0) + X_σ

presumes an isotropic path-loss exponent (n ≈ 2.0 - 2.5) and stationary zero-mean Gaussian shadowing (X_σ). In physical buildings with steel studs, HVAC ducts, and human bodies, the true effective exponent fluctuates non-isotropically between 1.4 and 4.2. Classical LDPL achieved a Test MAE of 3.2721 meters. Machine learning feature conditioning reduces position error by 61.1%, demonstrating the necessity of empirical non-linear regression for indoor radio telemetry.

7.0 Operational Engineering Directives & Topological Deployment

Authoritative Guidelines for Physical RTLS Anchor Installation

Directive 1: Vertical Mounting Height Vector (1.8m – 2.2m AFF)

Anchor beacons must be affixed between 1.8 and 2.2 meters Above Finished Floor (AFF). Mounting beacons below 1.0m subjects signals to destructive floor ground-plane reflection and human leg shadowing. Mounting flush against metallic ceiling trays causes severe electromagnetic backscattering. A mounting height of 2.0m optimizes line-of-sight clearance over desks and obstacles.

Directive 2: Structural Clearance Boundary (≥ 50 cm)

Maintain a strict minimum radial standoff clearance of 50 cm from reinforced concrete pillars, metallic filing cabinets, and electrical conduits. High-dielectric concrete and conductive metal deform beacon antenna radiation patterns, introducing localized multi-path nulls of 8–15 dBm.

Directive 3: Temporal Buffer Sizing (ω = 1000 ms, 10 Hz Advertising)

Configure client telemetry daemons with a sliding window buffer duration of ω = 1000 ms and an advertising rate of f_adv ≥ 10 Hz (100 ms interval). This ensures that every window contains ≥ 8 clean packet observations, guaranteeing statistical convergence for the 10-window rolling filters.

Directive 4: Spatial Cell Pitch (5m – 7m Triangular Mesh)

Deploy anchor beacons on a staggered triangular grid with cell pitch L ∈ [5m, 7m]. As demonstrated in Section 4, distance accuracy remains within sub-half-meter bounds up to 4.5 meters. Inter-beacon spacing of 6m ensures that any client tag is within the high-precision Line-of-Sight zone of at least three anchors simultaneously.

Directive 5: Realistic Spatial Fidelity Expectations

BLE RSSI localization is mathematically suited for room-level and quadrant-level tracking (55.4% within 1.0 meter). It cannot deliver millimeter-precision mechanical tracking due to physical multi-path constraints. Use-cases requiring millimeter tolerances must integrate Ultra-Wideband (UWB) Time-of-Flight or optical telemetry.
